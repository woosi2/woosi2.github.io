Curvature-Induced Leaflet Asymmetry in Flip-Flop and Phase Behavior of Lipid Vesicles

Jul 2024 – Present

Identified curvature-induced leaflet asymmetry in cholesterol flip-flop dynamics and phase behavior using coarse-grained MD simulations. Analyzed leaflet-specific structural properties, cholesterol flip-flop dynamics, and free-energy profiles across varying cholesterol concentrations.

Methods / Focus: Coarse-grained MD (Martini) · Area per molecule · P2 order parameter · Cholesterol flip-flop · Free-energy profiles

  • Papers

In small vesicles, the high curvature makes the inner and outer leaflets differ in area and lipid packing. Using coarse-grained Martini simulations of DPPC/cholesterol vesicles, I study how this curvature induces asymmetry in cholesterol flip-flop dynamics and phase behavior between the two leaflets.

Hybrid Monte Carlo–Molecular Dynamics for Reliable Lipid Vesicle Equilibration

Mar 2025 – Present

Developed a hybrid MC–MD method using interleaflet lipid swaps for efficient equilibration of ternary lipid vesicles. Evaluated the traditional pore equilibration method by analyzing the convergence of leaflet composition, lipid numbers, and internal water content, and validated equilibrium properties against hybrid MC–MD.

Methods / Focus: Hybrid MC–MD · Interleaflet lipid swaps · Metropolis criterion · GROMACS 2025 · Leaflet composition convergence

  • Papers

Building on the asymmetry found in binary vesicles, I am extending my research to more complex ternary vesicles known to form raft-like domains. Interpreting phase behavior in these systems requires well-defined equilibrium conditions. In molecular dynamics, however, phospholipid flip-flop is far slower than accessible simulation times, so the leaflet composition of a vesicle stays close to the initial guess made when the system is built.

To address this, I am developing a vesicle equilibration methodology based on hybrid Monte Carlo–molecular dynamics (MC–MD). Periodically during MD, Monte Carlo moves swap lipids between the inner and outer leaflets and are accepted or rejected with the Metropolis criterion based on the energy change, allowing the leaflet composition to relax on timescales that plain MD cannot reach. The method is implemented directly in GROMACS 2025, and the code will be released publicly.

Microscopic Fatigue Mechanisms in Metallic Glasses Using Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials

Mar 2026 – Present

Developed a DFT-trained Moment Tensor Potential (MTP) for Zr–Ni–Al metallic glasses across a wide range of compositions, validating structural and dynamical properties. Currently investigating microscopic fatigue mechanisms under cyclic loading by analyzing atomic-scale correlations between local structure and non-affine deformation.

Methods / Focus: DFT · Moment Tensor Potential · Cyclic-loading MD · Icosahedral cluster networks · Non-affine deformation

Metallic glasses combine high strength with the absence of crystalline defects, yet their fatigue limits are low, and the general mechanism behind this remains unclear. Addressing this question requires simulations across a broad range of compositions with accuracy close to first-principles calculations.

To this end, I developed a machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP) for Zr–Ni–Al metallic glasses trained on DFT data. Using MD simulations of cyclic loading, I am now studying how local atomic structure, such as icosahedral clusters and their networks, relates to atomic rearrangements, to uncover the microscopic mechanisms of fatigue in metallic glasses.
